Method

Built for use case KJS-CES-01, Climate Intelligence for Heatwave Monitoring, Prediction and Early Warning (IMD Mumbai–Pune). The pipeline below runs end to end with python src/train.py.

  1. Load 25 years of daily weather

  2. Label days with IMD criteria

    Normal Tmax per city and calendar day comes from 2000–2020 only, smoothed over 31 days. A day is a heatwave when Tmax ≥ 40 °C (37 °C on the coast) and is at least 4.5 °C above normal, or when Tmax ≥ 45 °C. Severe: departure above 6.4 °C or Tmax ≥ 47 °C.

  3. Frame it as a next-day warning

    Each row uses what is known by the evening of day t to predict day t + 1. Only March to July target days are kept, the IMD heatwave season.

  4. Engineer 12 features

    Departure from normal, the 3-day mean departure and trend, the IMD margin (how close tomorrow would be to the criteria if today's anomaly persists), feels-like temperature, diurnal range, 7-day rain, wind, tomorrow's normal and a coastal flag.

  5. Train the SVM with an RBF kernel

  6. Test on unseen years

    2021–2024, including the record 2024 season, is scored once. Logistic regression, a linear SVM and persistence are compared against it on the same features.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The data is gridded reanalysis from Open-Meteo, which reads about 1–2 °C cooler than IMD station thermometers on the hottest days. Event counts here are conservative.
  • Labels are applied per city. IMD also requires two stations in a sub-division on two consecutive days before it declares a heatwave.
  • With only today's observations, the first day of a spell is hard to call. Adding numerical weather prediction inputs (forecast Tmax) would raise onset recall the most.
  • Probabilities come from Platt scaling and are reliable for ranking. Treat values between 10 % and 50 % as a watch rather than a precise likelihood.

IMD limit and normal temperature for each city

The IMD limit is 40 °C for inland cities and 37 °C for coastal ones. The normal is the average top temperature for that date over 2000–2020, smoothed over the 15 days either side so a single odd year doesn't skew it. Values below are for the 15th of each month.
